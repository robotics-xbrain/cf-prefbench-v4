# 6 Discussion

## 6.1 Why the Cliff Exists

The lexical cliff has two empirically distinguishable mechanisms in our experiments. On motion verbs, accuracy decreases monotonically with the held-out verb's CLIP-text cosine to training, with a transition zone around cosine $0.92$–$0.94$ on ViT-B/32 (Section~4.2, Pearson $r{=}0.808$). The 4-verb curve, including the boundary-zone token `scoot` we added in Phase 5 Exp~3, is consistent with a smooth sigmoid in cosine. On size and speed, held-out tokens within the same semantic class cluster at nearly-identical cosines (within $0.01$) but accuracy is determined by class membership rather than by cosine: BIG and SLOW classes pass at $0.83$–$0.93$; SMALL and FAST classes collapse to $0.04$–$0.49$ (Section~4.4). We interpret the asymmetry as a default-class bias learned at training time — on size, "pick the larger block" is the safer default given the training distribution's visual salience pattern; on speed, "pick the slower video" is the safer default because the model has more frames to decide on. Held-out tokens in the default class succeed because the default is correct for them; held-out tokens in the opposite class fail because the lexical signal is too weak to override the default.

ViT-L/14 closes both mechanisms because its denser text embedding space resolves held-out paraphrases into stable attribute-attention queries even when their raw cosine to training is lower than under B/32 (motion transit: $0.500 \rightarrow 0.917$ at L/14 cosine $0.876$, lower than B/32's $0.915$). The partial closure on speed FAST ($0.115 \rightarrow 0.389$) shows scaling helps but is not universally sufficient; we hypothesize (Section~4.5) that the temporally-distributed speed signal makes the default-class bias more entrenched than for the spatially-localized size signal, but we do not isolate which of the two factors dominates and leave a controlled study for future work.

## 6.2 What VLM Baselines Reveal

The cross-method comparison in Section~4.4 establishes that the cliff is method-specific to LIB+ViT-B/32, not a property of vision-language models in general. GPT-4o nails the four cliff tokens on size SMALL and speed FAST that catastrophically fail under LIB+B/32 (Δ $+0.337$ to $+0.595$), confirming that nothing about the held-out tokens themselves makes them un-decodable. The cliff is in our pipeline's coupling between a small CLIP text embedding and a per-attribute attention head.

GPT-4o reveals a complementary failure mode: at $192 \times 144$ video resolution presented as a $2 \times 4$ image grid, it cannot reliably read direction tokens, scoring $0.48$–$0.60$ across all four motion verbs including the LIB above-cliff verbs where LIB scores $0.92$. This is a visual decoding limit, not a lexical limit. The Qwen2-VL-2B baseline sits near chance throughout the cliff matrix, providing a minimum-capability data-point. The picture that emerges is not "VLMs solve compositional preference" or "VLMs cliff like LIB-B/32" but rather: \emph{the best method varies by binding axis}, with LIB-L/14 dominating motion and GPT-4o dominating speed FAST. We argue this is the honest characterization of the current state-of-the-art on this benchmark.

## 6.3 Implications for Benchmark Design

Three practices follow directly from our findings. First, held-out lexical splits should accompany held-out video splits in compositional preference benchmarks. A benchmark that randomizes train/test on examples but reuses the same paraphrase verbs in both splits will not detect the cliff. CF-PrefBench v4's existing held-out lexical split caught the cliff because its paraphrase pool is disjoint from training; Exp~3 and Exp~4 strengthened it by adding cliff-zone tokens spanning cosine $0.87$–$0.97$.

Second, evaluation should be stratified by held-out token's CLIP cosine to training, not just by axis. The 4-verb cliff curve (Figure~4.1) and the cross-axis matrix (Figure~4.3) are more informative than scalar PFA, especially when a single failing token can cascade the strict-PFA aggregation to zero (footnote in Section~4.1). Reporting per-token row accuracy alongside flip-group PFA is necessary to interpret aggregate failures.

Third, the sanity diagnostic battery (Section~5) should be run before publishing positive results on any architecture with multiple signal pathways. The battery converts noisy NORMAL-accuracy rankings into unambiguous falsifications of pass-through, gate triviality, and instruction non-use — modes that produce indistinguishable accuracy curves under standard evaluation.

## 6.4 Limitations

We probe three of v4's seven binding axes (motion\_sequence, size, speed). The four remaining axes (color, object, action, spatial) are not tested here; whether they exhibit cliffs is left to future work. The choice of these three was driven by the availability of cliff-zone held-out tokens with semantic-class structure. Color and spatial held-outs are tuples (magenta$\leftrightarrow$cyan, north$\leftrightarrow$south) whose structure differs from size/speed.

We compare two backbone scales (B/32, L/14); intermediate scales (B/16) and alternative backbones (DINOv2, EVA-02) are unaddressed. Whether the cliff transition with backbone scale is gradual or sharp is an open question; a fuller scaling study would require six or more CLIP variants. The speedily L/14 mean has variance $\sigma = 0.230$ (one seed reaches $0.786$, the other two stay at $0.286$–$0.310$); this single-seed effect is reported as a borderline case in Section~4.6 and not used as load-bearing evidence.

The sanity battery in Section~5 was designed \emph{after} we caught the Phase 3 false positive on our own architecture, not before. The chronological ordering is important: the battery is a corrective rather than a predictive tool, and its current design reflects the specific failure modes we encountered (centroid pass-through, gate triviality, instruction non-use). Other failure modes — for instance, train-test split contamination at the video level, or model memorization of paraphrase IDs — are not directly addressed and require complementary probes. CF-PrefBench v4 uses a deterministic 2D rendered simulator; the cliff and the battery's diagnostic power on it may be specific to its structured visual distribution. Replication on natural-video preference datasets remains future work. Finally, we do not report inter-annotator agreement on the preferred labels because v4 labels are deterministically derived from the simulator state, not from human judgment. Whether human annotators would produce the same labels — and whether the cliff transfers to human-labeled compositional preference data — is a critical open question.

## 6.5 Future Work

Three directions follow naturally. First, cross-domain validation: apply the cliff probe and sanity battery to instruction-following preference data (e.g., Anthropic HH-RLHF, OpenAssistant) where the held-out verb structure differs and visual decoding is absent. If a cliff appears in text-only LMs at small scale and closes at larger scale, the mechanism we describe is not specific to vision-language coupling. Second, prescriptive interventions: the battery is currently diagnostic. Whether targeted data augmentation, contrastive lexical training, or backbone fine-tuning can close the cliff on a fixed backbone is unknown. Third, the partial closure on speed FAST under L/14 suggests that backbone scaling is not uniformly sufficient. Pinpointing whether the temporal binding signal or the default-class bias is the dominant obstacle would clarify when scaling helps and when other interventions are needed.

## 6.6 Conclusion

We report two findings on CF-PrefBench v4. First, a small CLIP backbone (ViT-B/32) coupled to a per-attribute attention head exhibits a lexical cliff: held-out paraphrases that drift outside the training-verb CLIP cluster lose binding accuracy systematically (motion: smooth sigmoid in cosine; size/speed: class-bimodal default bias). The cliff is closed by ViT-L/14 on motion and size, and partially closed on speed. Second, the cliff is method-specific to LIB+B/32 — zero-shot GPT-4o handles the size and speed cliff tokens correctly while failing on motion direction-binding at low resolution. As a methodological contribution we present a six-probe sanity battery that caught a centroid pass-through in our own earlier architecture and falsified four anti-collapse mechanisms. We recommend held-out lexical splits, cosine-stratified evaluation, and routine sanity reporting as standard practice for compositional preference research.
